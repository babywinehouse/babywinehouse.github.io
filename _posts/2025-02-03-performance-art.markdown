In one of my jobs, I do storytimes for children and at first it was nerve-wracking getting up in front of the families. I could prepare all I wanted, stories and songs I would sing, put them all in a list, and then get out there and just do something entirely different. It felt scary and brought out a part of me that felt terrified I'd done a 'bad job'. And every session is different, and sometimes all my jokes really don't land, and I run out of ideas or confuse everyone with a metaphor no one asked for.

But it was a rush--and I realised that I love performing! It's something I haven't done in a long time. In my teen years and early adulthood I considered myself a bit of a performance artist. At school, Drama had been my top subject and my passion at and beyond school. But there were some politics in the drama world that I eventually stepped away from to pursue a more academic pathway in Gender Studies. That was a period where I considered myself a writer, with no less of an emphasis on writing as art, as craft, and as sculpture, as I considered performing.

And it is so interesting to me to have returned to a place of performing in a context that feels so natural to me now that I've been doing it for a little while. I love to have a crowd, to chat to the crowd and include the edges of the crowd and get everybody doing the same thing together. That's where I think the real magic is of performing, is in everyone joining in to create something bigger than ourselves. And so lovely to centre the littlest people in creating magic together. 

So I sing (always) and I play instruments (the ukulele, not well) and I dance and shout and whisper and laugh. And I feel alive, and I help make other people feel alive. And that is really incredible to a younger version of me, cheering me on. That I've found a way to do all of these things that bring me joy, and do them with lots of people coming together. What an honour and an accomplishment. 

And I know that I won't do this job forever, but it will live in my heart forever. And while it lasts I will savour every bit of fun I get to have in a room where everyone's looking at me and the point is to engage those little peoples' brains and bodies and spirits to grow and learn and dance. 

And that's part of my story about coming back to dance as well, that I want to find new opportunities to be a performer and to dance with others. I'm keen to give Bring A Plate Dance another go as well, and to have that community feel in performing. 

But I also want to have some act of my own, whether it is dancing, singing, a skit, or some combination (likely). This little blog is probably part of that too, but it gets complicated when my writing ego gets in the way! I just want to put out as much as I can of my own writing and see what sticks and what creates momentum for me creatively. Learning in public, if you will.
